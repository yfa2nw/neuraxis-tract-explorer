How to Use Neuraxis Tract Explorer

Choose a pathway, cranial nerve, brain structure or lesion, and the explorer shows where it runs through the brain and spinal cord: on the cross-sections, the 3D brain and the side-on schematic, with clinical notes in the info panel.

Quick start

  1. Point at any item in the list on the left to preview it. Click it to pin it in place. You can also click a structure on a slice.
  2. Read the info panel at the top right: where it starts and ends, where it crosses the midline, what it does and what damage looks like.
  3. Follow it through the slices below. Only the levels it passes through are shown; use ← Prev Slice / Next Slice → to step along it. Press Esc or Unpin ✕ to start again.

Find something

  • Press / and type a name, abbreviation or syndrome, for example Wallenberg, INO or nucleus ambiguus.
  • The chips under the search box (Sensory, Motor, Cerebellar, Cranial nerves, Lesions) narrow the list.
  • The level menu lists only what passes through one level.

Read a slice

  • The back of the brain (dorsal) is at the top and the front (ventral) at the bottom.
  • ▲ ascending (sensory), ▼ descending (motor), ⇅ both directions.
  • ✕ and a dashed line mark where a pathway crosses the midline (a decussation).
  • The left half is the side the pathway starts on (ipsilateral), the right half the opposite side (contralateral). Pathway Side highlights the side it is on at each level; Bilateral lights both.

Lesions and strokes

  • Open the Lesions chip, or the Spinal cord lesions, Cerebral strokes and Brainstem strokes groups in the list.
  • The shaded area is the lesion. Structures in red are damaged; amber ones may be partly affected.
  • The info panel gives the cause, blood supply, signs, bedside tests (How to check), imaging and other names.
  • For a one-sided lesion, Left half / Right half flips the side.

3D brain and schematic

  • Drag to rotate the 3D brain; scroll or use − / + to zoom, and Reset to recentre.
  • Lower 3D Opacity to see the deep structures.
  • The 2D schematic traces the selected tract from its start to its end, with ✕ where it crosses the midline; the card underneath spells out each step and the route.
  • Click a level in the 2D schematic, or a slice plane in 3D, to jump to that slice.

Change the view

  • Slice Mode: a clean drawing (Vector) or a stain- or MRI-style rendering underneath it (Weigert Stain, MRI, Luxol Blue). These are illustrations, not photographs of real tissue.
  • ⤢ Spotlight (or a slice title) enlarges a slice; click again to shrink it.
  • Active Slices Only hides levels the pinned item does not reach; Show All 12 Slices brings them back.
  • Drag the edge of any panel or slice to resize it (double-click to reset). The Grid slider sets the slice size.

Test yourself

  • 🎓 Quiz Me! can ask you to identify structures on the slices (Multiple Choice, Type the Name, Label the Slice), work out a lesion from symptoms or from its shading, match a lesion to its presentation, or link structures and functions.
  • Pick the levels and number of questions; each answer comes with an explanation.

A study aid, not medical advice: positions on the slices are schematic, so check them against your own references.
